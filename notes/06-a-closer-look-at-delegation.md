# 06. A closer look at Delegation

Video: ~6 min

## Learning objectives
- Understand Delegation and its three components: **Problem Awareness, Platform Awareness, Task Delegation**
- Recognise when and how to delegate to AI effectively
- Develop awareness of task, platform and mode considerations

## Where Delegation fits
- Of the four aims of AI Fluency (effective, efficient, ethical, safe), Delegation is **primarily about the first two: effective and efficient**.
- Core question: **what work is to be done, what should you do yourself, and what is better suited to AI?**
- Sounds simple but is **surprisingly nuanced**. It requires knowing both what you are trying to accomplish and what you and the AI can each realistically do.
- It is about **breaking complex work into manageable pieces** and deciding who handles which part, whether through **automation, augmentation or agency**.

## The surprise: good delegation is not mainly about AI
> The cornerstone of good delegation is **your own expertise** and your understanding of what you are trying to accomplish.

> "The most effective AI collaborators are **experts in their fields first and AI delegators second**."

## Component 1: Problem Awareness
*The ability to clearly define your goals and understand what work is needed **before** bringing AI in.*

Questions to answer first:
- What exactly are you trying to accomplish, create or solve?
- What is your vision, what are the key project goals?
- **What does success look like** to you?
- What kind of thinking and work is needed to get there? Look for:
  - areas that are **simple but time-consuming**
  - areas of **uncertainty** where a trusted thinking partner would help
  - areas of **ignorance** where you need more data
  - areas needing **critical judgment**

Every project and domain differs; the course cannot make you an expert in your problem space. But **without a clear understanding of goals and the work involved, even the most advanced AI will not get you there**. You will struggle to work with AI, **evaluate its outputs**, or know **when human intervention is needed**.

## Component 2: Platform Awareness
*A working knowledge of available AI systems and their specific capabilities and limitations.*

- Different systems offer **vastly different capabilities**, and the field changes almost daily.
- Delegation is **not about finding one perfect system**; it is about knowing the strengths and limits of the options available.
- Questions:
  - Have you checked **which models perform best** for the kind of work you have in mind?
  - Do you know which systems prioritise **speed over depth**, or **accuracy over creativity**?
- Best approach: **hands-on**. Experiment with different systems as often as you can and build insight from personal experience.

## Component 3: Task Delegation
*The strategic process of dividing work between humans and AI to use the strengths of each.*

Questions:
- Which parts of the workflow would benefit from **automation**?
- Where would **augmentation** create more value than either working alone or fully automating?
- Are there **critical judgment areas that should stay exclusively human** and not be delegated at all?
- What **routine interactions can AI agents handle** on your behalf (agency)?

Outcome: an informed decision for each part of the work, one of four buckets:
1. **Automated** by AI
2. **Human-AI collaboration** (augmentation)
3. **Reserved exclusively for humans**
4. **Handled by AI agents** acting on your behalf

### Diagram: the delegation flow
```
 1. PROBLEM AWARENESS      2. PLATFORM AWARENESS      3. TASK DELEGATION
 ─────────────────────     ──────────────────────     ──────────────────────────

```

## Recap
| Component | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Problem Awareness | Understand your goals and the problem |
| Platform Awareness | Understand AI capabilities and limitations |
| Task Delegation | Strategically divide the work |

- Effective collaboration is **not "handing over the wheel and calling it a day"**. It is about thoughtful choices that use the unique strengths of human and artificial intelligence.
- The goal is **not to automate everything** but to create the most effective human-AI partnership for the task.
- Requires **both domain expertise and an understanding of AI capabilities**.

## Exercise: Analyse a task with an AI assistant
1. Pick a small task (draft an email, outline a presentation, plan a meeting or event).
2. Start a chat with Claude, e.g. *"Hi Claude, I'm preparing how to [task] and want to discuss with you what a delegation plan may look like for figuring out which parts I should delegate to an AI like you vs. not. Can you help me with this?"*
3. Explore together:
   - What is the overall **vision**? What does a **good result** look like?
   - What are the different **bits of work** needed?
   - Which bits need **human expertise, creativity or judgment**?
4. **Have a real back-and-forth conversation**, not just lists of statements. Each of you may see something the other does not.
5. Build a simple **delegation plan** using both your strengths and the AI's.
