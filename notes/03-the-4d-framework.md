# 03. The 4D Framework

Video: ~5 min · Presenter: Rick Dakan (Ringling College of Art and Design)

## Learning objective
- Explain the AI Fluency Framework and its four core competencies, the **4Ds**: Delegation, Description, Discernment, Diligence

## Framing
- Whatever mode you are in (automation, augmentation or agency), **four competencies make all the difference** to collaborating effectively, efficiently, ethically and safely.

| D | One-line definition | Main purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Delegation** | Thoughtfully deciding what work to do with AI vs. yourself | effective + efficient |
| **Description** | Communicating clearly with AI systems | collaborative success |
| **Discernment** | Evaluating AI outputs and behaviour with a critical eye | quality, usefulness |
| **Diligence** | Interacting with AI responsibly | safe + ethical |

### Diagram: how the 4Ds fit together
```
 ┌─────────────── DILIGENCE: around everything (ethical + safe) ───────────────┐
 │                                                                             │
 │  DELEGATION ──► DESCRIPTION ──► AI output ──► DISCERNMENT                   │
 │  what goes      tell it                       is it good?                   │
 │  to AI?         clearly                            │                        │
 │      ▲              ▲                              │                        │
 │      │              └───────── refine ◄────────────┤  (the D-D loop)        │
 │      └──────────── wrong tool / approach ◄─────────┘                        │
 │                                                                             │
 └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
   Delegation, Description, Discernment: mainly effective + efficient
```

## 1. Delegation: the big picture
Questions to ask:
- What are you trying to accomplish?
- What kinds of work are involved?
- What should you handle yourself, and where might AI help?

Example (research project): have the AI review lengthy documents and data, then have a thoughtful discussion with it about implications and findings, but **reserve the critical analysis and final conclusions for yourself**.

To delegate well you need to:
1. **Understand your goal** and the problem you are solving
2. **Recognise what AI can and cannot do well**
3. **Thoughtfully divide the work** between you and the AI

Delegation is **not just offloading tasks**. It is having a clear vision and **strategically choosing how AI fits into your process**. Essential for effective *and* efficient collaboration.

## 2. Description: clear communication
- Contrast: vaguely saying *"Make me a logo"* vs. describing your company's **values, target audience, preferred colours, style references**, etc.
- Tutor example: *"Don't tell me the answer, just help me work through this problem step by step so I can better understand the concept."* This describes **how you want the AI to behave**, not just what you want.
- Description goes beyond writing prompts. It means **detailed, context-rich conversations** that establish:
  - what you hope to achieve and the **format of the output**
  - **how the AI should approach** the task
  - the **context and information** it needs to work with you well
  - the **tone and style** of the interaction
- Goal: articulate your needs and vision so that **both you and the AI** are set up for collaborative success.

## 3. Discernment: evaluating what AI gives you
Example: you asked for a marketing strategy. Assess:
- Are the **facts accurate**?
- Does the **reasoning make sense**?
- Do the recommendations **align with your brand values and audience**?
- Most importantly: **does the output actually help you move forward?**

Discernment:
- draws on **your own domain expertise**
- needs judgment and critical insight to **separate the useful from the not useful**
- includes recognising when output **needs refinement** or should be **set aside entirely**

**Description-Discernment loop:** most AI interactions are small loops of *describe what you need → evaluate what you get → refine the request → repeat*. (Covered in depth in lesson 11.)

## 4. Diligence: responsible interaction
Example questions:
- Using AI to write job descriptions or review applications: how do you **ensure fairness and control for bias**?
- Making important decisions with AI help: how do you **verify the accuracy** of what it presents?
- Are you **protecting sensitive data**?
- Have you considered how to be **transparent about AI's involvement**?
- Are you **willing to be accountable** for the AI-assisted work?

Diligence = **taking ownership** of AI-assisted work and being willing to **stand behind the final product**. Critical for safe and ethical collaboration.

## Recap
- AI Fluency = practical skills, knowledge, insights and values for using AI effectively, efficiently, ethically and safely.
- Delegation decides **when and how** to use AI · Description **communicates** clearly · Discernment **evaluates** outputs · Diligence uses AI **responsibly**.
- Why they matter: they are **not tied to specific tools or techniques** that go out of date. They are fundamental skills that let you adapt as the technology evolves.
- The 4Ds apply across **all three** modes (automation, augmentation, agency).

## Exercises
**Exercise 1: Apply the 4Ds (5 min).** Pick a scenario and answer the four questions:

| Scenario | Delegation | Description | Discernment | Diligence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Communication:** AI drafts a series of marketing campaign emails | What do you handle vs. collaborate on? | How do you convey tone, purpose, success criteria? | What criteria tell you the drafts meet your needs? | What transparency/responsibility issues matter? |
| **Research:** AI helps analyse a large dataset for a paper | How do you split the analytical work? | What context about your research question does the AI need? | How do you verify its analysis? | What ethical issues arise when publishing AI-assisted research? |
| **Creative:** AI helps develop story character concepts | What do you explore with AI vs. develop alone? | How do you guide it to fit your story's world? | How do you decide what to keep, modify, discard? | How do you acknowledge AI's contribution? |

**Exercise 2: Explore something you love (5 to 10 min).** Chat naturally with Claude about a topic you know well. Notice when Claude **enhances your thinking**, when you need to **clarify or correct** it, and when **your expertise** leads you to evaluate its responses. (Tip from the page: try the course exercises with several assistants to feel how they differ.)

**Exercise 3: Learn something new (5 to 10 min).** Ask Claude to teach you an unfamiliar topic; do not worry about prompting "right". Notice helpful explanations, concrete examples, natural responses to follow-ups, and **places where you would want to double-check**. Compare with Exercise 2: discernment is much harder when you lack expertise.
