# 01. Introduction to AI Fluency

Video: ~4 min · Module time: 10 to 15 min
Presenters: Joseph Feller (Cork University Business School), Drew Bent (teacher, programmer, member of technical staff at Anthropic), Rick Dakan (Ringling College of Art and Design), Maggie Vo (education research team, Anthropic)

## Learning objectives
- Understand the purpose and structure of the course
- Recognise why AI Fluency matters today
- Identify the key components of the learning journey ahead
- Set clear expectations for what the course will give you

## What the course is (and is not) about
- The course is **not really about AI as a technology**. It is about **us humans** and how we interact and collaborate with AI systems.
- The aim is to imagine AI as more than a "next-level spell checker": as a **trusted partner for creative and innovative problem solving**, and to approach that confidently and capably.
- The AI world moves very fast. Without a **grounding framework** it is hard to know where to start or keep up, or to get past "the next prompt engineering fad". That is the reason the course exists.
- It is explicitly **not** a collection of technical definitions or prompting tips "that may be outdated next month".
- Core goal: **change how we think *about* AI so that we can learn how to think *with* AI.**

## Definition: AI Fluency
> The ability to engage with AI systems in ways that are **effective, efficient, ethical, and safe**.

## Why now
- Over the last few years AI went from specialised technology to interactive systems that millions use daily at school, work and home.
- This creates both **opportunity and uncertainty**. The presenters observe:
  - organisations adopting AI **without clear strategies**
  - people **growing frustrated** with systems they do not fully understand
  - a **widening gap between what is possible and what feels comfortable and intuitive**
- The course is meant to help bridge that gap.

## Course structure (the journey)
1. Overview of the **AI Fluency Framework**: a model of human-AI interaction the authors found useful and powerful. It covers the *ways* we interact with AI and the **four core competencies (the 4Ds)** that make those interactions richer.
2. A closer look at each competency, each framed as a question:
   - **Delegation**: when should humans do the work, and when should AI?
   - **Description**: how do we communicate clearly with AI systems?
   - **Discernment**: how do we evaluate what AI gives us?
   - **Diligence**: how do we make sure our interaction with AI is responsible, transparent and accountable?
3. Each section has focused videos plus exercises. You are encouraged to spend much of the course **actually trying things**.
4. Anthropic staff also cover: what makes today's AI different from earlier technologies, real capabilities and limitations, and practical techniques that apply the 4Ds.

## The teaching philosophy
- Most AI training focuses only on **tactical skills** (specific prompts, which settings to adjust, which system to use). Useful, but these **go out of date quickly**.
- This course focuses on **core competencies and deep understanding**, so you are prepared for today's AI *and* the changes to come.

## What you will have by the end
1. A **framework** to guide your own interactions with AI
2. **Confidence** in deciding when and how to engage AI effectively
3. **Practical skills** for more thoughtful, fluid human-AI collaboration
4. The ability to **evaluate and take responsibility** for the outcomes of those collaborations (the page phrases it as evaluating and disseminating AI-assisted work responsibly)

## Key takeaways (from the lesson page)
- The focus is human-AI **collaboration**, not just understanding AI as a technology
- AI Fluency = effective, efficient, ethical, safe
- The framework centres on the 4Ds: Delegation, Description, Discernment, Diligence
- The goal is **lasting** skills that stay relevant as AI evolves
- Effective collaboration needs practical skills **and** a fundamental shift in how you think about working with AI
